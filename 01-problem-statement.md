# Problem Statement

## Official theme

**Problem Statement 6.3 — Home-based and micro food units (pickles, snacks, spices, millets and flour mills) struggle to meet requirements for selling beyond their town.**

## In simple words

1. **Limited market access:** Many producers, including women-led home businesses, rely on WhatsApp, local fairs and nearby customers.
2. **Difficult compliance and records:** Registration, appropriate labels, batch traceability and evidence for shelf-life declarations can be difficult to manage informally.
3. **Unclear pricing:** Without costing ingredients, labour, packaging and delivery accurately, producers may underprice their products and struggle to scale.

## Why this matters

Retail buyers and e-commerce channels may request documents, labels, traceability and commercial information that local informal selling processes have not required producers to organise systematically. These expectations may include both **law** and **individual buyer policy**, which must be distinguished.

## Project objective

Develop an accessible, guided system that can help a micro producer **prepare a documented product listing and identify the remaining gaps**, without claiming to provide legal certification or guaranteed buyer acceptance.

## Target users

- Micro food processors producing pickles, snacks, spices, millet foods or similar packaged products.
- Home-based entrepreneurs and self-help groups, including women-led enterprises.
- Producer-support organisations assisting business formalisation.

## Illustrative pilot

A Karnataka mango-pickle producer wanting to move from local WhatsApp orders toward retailer or e-commerce sales. This is a **demo scenario**, not a completed user interview or field pilot.

## What is outside this problem

We do not claim that every producer is unregistered, all informal labels are illegal, or every platform applies identical buyer requirements. Exact obligations depend on facts, applicable rules, and the chosen sales channel.
